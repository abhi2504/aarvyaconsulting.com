Why is money cut from your salary? TDS, explained simply

Your CTC is ₹10L, your bank gets ₹8L. Where did ₹2L go? Here's the answer in plain English.

🪙 In 60 seconds
  • TDS = Tax Deducted at Source. The government's way of collecting income tax in real-time, instead of waiting till year-end.
  • Your employer cuts a slice of your salary every month and deposits it as tax in your name.
  • You don't lose it — it's pre-paid tax. At year-end you adjust it against your final tax liability. Excess comes back as refund.
  • Visible in Form 16 (employer-issued) and Form 26AS (government record).

The simple story

Imagine the government as a landlord. You pay rent (income tax) once a year — but the landlord doesn't trust you to save up. So instead, your employer (the broker) automatically cuts a portion of your rent every month and gives it to the landlord. That's TDS.

You haven't "paid extra". You've pre-paid. At year-end, when you file your ITR, the system tallies what you owed vs what was already deposited. Difference = refund (excess) or balance due (shortfall).

How the flow works

flowchart LR
  Employer[Employer / Bank / Client] -->|Pays you net amount| You
  Employer -->|Deposits TDS| Govt[Income Tax Dept]
  Govt -->|Records under your PAN| Form26AS[Your Form 26AS / AIS]
  You -->|Files ITR| Adjust[Tax owed vs TDS paid]
  Adjust -->|Excess| Refund[Refund to your bank]
  Adjust -->|Shortfall| Pay[Pay balance tax]

Who deducts TDS?

  • Your employer — on salary (Section 192)
  • Your bank — on FD interest above ₹40,000/year (₹50,000 for seniors) under Section 194A
  • Your tenant — if rent > ₹50,000/month (Section 194-IB)
  • Your clients — if you're a freelancer/professional, on payments above ₹30,000 (194J) or ₹40,000 (194C contracts)
  • Buyer of property — 1% TDS on purchase over ₹50 lakh (194-IA)
  • Companies paying dividend — 10% above ₹5,000 (194)

How much does the employer cut?

Your employer estimates your total annual tax (based on the regime you picked + declared investments) and divides it across 12 months.

Example: gross salary ₹10L, expected annual tax ₹46,800 → ~₹3,900/month TDS.

💡
Why TDS jumps in MarchIf you forget to submit investment proofs (LIC receipts, rent receipts, ELSS, etc.) on time, your employer assumes ₹0 deductions early in the year and catches up by cutting big TDS in February/March. Submit proofs by January to avoid the squeeze.

How to claim TDS back / verify it

  1. Check Form 26AS / AIS

    Go to incometax.gov.in → e-File → View Form 26AS / AIS. Every TDS deduction in your PAN should show up here. Step-by-step here.

  2. Match with Form 16 / 16A

    Form 16 = TDS on salary (from employer). Form 16A = TDS on non-salary payments (from banks, clients). The figures must match 26AS.

  3. File ITR claiming the TDS

    While filing ITR, the system auto-pulls TDS from 26AS. It offsets against your computed tax. Excess → refund.

⚠️
If 26AS doesn't show TDS that was actually cutEither your PAN was missing/wrong, or the deductor hasn't deposited yet. Contact them immediately — without 26AS entry, you can't claim it. Wait 30–45 days after quarter-end before raising a grievance.

TDS isn't a "tax". It's a deposit in your name. The whole skill of tax season is making sure every rupee deducted lands in your 26AS — and gets adjusted in your return.
