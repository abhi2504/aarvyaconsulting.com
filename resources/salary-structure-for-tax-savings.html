Salary structure for max tax savings

Two people earning ₹15L can pay very different tax — depending on how their CTC is split. Here's the structure that wins.

🪙 In 60 seconds
  • Components that save tax: HRA, LTA, NPS employer contribution, food coupons, car perquisite, books/periodicals reimbursement.
  • Most tax-efficient salaries combine 40% basic + HRA + NPS + reimbursements + variable component.
  • These savings only work under Old Regime (except NPS 80CCD(2) and standard deduction which also work in New).
  • Negotiate at offer / appraisal stage — mid-year changes are hard.

HRA — the biggest single saver

HRA exemption (Section 10(13A)) = least of three:

  1. Actual HRA received
  2. 50% of basic (metro) or 40% (non-metro)
  3. Actual rent paid minus 10% of basic

Example

Basic ₹6L. HRA ₹3L received. Rent paid ₹25K/month (₹3L/yr) in Mumbai.

  • HRA received: ₹3,00,000
  • 50% of basic: ₹3,00,000
  • Rent − 10% basic: 3,00,000 − 60,000 = ₹2,40,000

Exemption = ₹2,40,000. Tax saved at 30% slab = ₹74,880.

💡
Can you claim HRA if you live with parents?Yes. Pay rent to your parents (via bank transfer). Get rent receipts. Parents declare it as their income (still tax-free up to their exemption limit). Win-win — but it must be a genuine arrangement.

LTA — twice in 4 years

LTA exemption (Section 10(5)) — for travel within India, actual expense up to allowance limit. Two trips allowed in a 4-year block (current block 2022-25).

  • Only covers travel cost (flight / train / bus) for self + family
  • Doesn't cover lodging, food
  • Need original tickets / boarding passes
  • Family = spouse, children, dependent siblings, dependent parents

Food coupons / meal vouchers

  • Exempt: ₹50 per meal × 2 meals per working day
  • Typical annual: ~₹26,400 (₹2,200/month)
  • Issued by employer through Sodexo, Zaggle, etc.
  • Use them — they expire monthly typically

NPS employer contribution (80CCD(2)) — best modern saver

  • Employer can contribute up to 10% of (basic + DA) to NPS Tier 1
  • Fully exempt from tax — over and above the ₹1.5L Section 80C cap
  • Works in BOTH old and new regime
  • Funds lock in till 60, then 40% mandatory annuity

For basic ₹6L → up to ₹60K/year in tax-free NPS employer contribution. Tax saved at 30% slab = ₹18,720/year.

Reimbursements (with bills)

These are fully tax-free if backed by bills:

  • Mobile / internet for office use
  • Books, periodicals, professional development
  • Uniform allowance (if office mandates)
  • Driver's salary (for company car)
  • Fuel reimbursement (for office travel — different from regular commute)

Car / vehicle perquisite

If employer provides car:

  • For official use only: nil perquisite
  • For mixed use: ₹1,800/month (≤ 1.6L cc) or ₹2,400/month (> 1.6L cc) — far less than actual benefit
  • Plus driver: ₹900/month flat

This is a significant under-valuation. For senior roles, company car is a major tax perk.

What to negotiate at offer time

  • Keep Basic at ~40% of CTC (lower = lower HRA exemption + lower retirement benefits)
  • Get HRA structured as 50% of basic (metro) / 40% (non-metro)
  • Ask for employer NPS contribution (80CCD(2)) — up to 10% of basic
  • Get LTA in your salary structure
  • Food coupons + phone/internet reimbursement options
  • If senior role: ask for company car

Two people with the same CTC can have ₹50,000–₹2,00,000 difference in net pay — purely based on how the salary is structured. Negotiate the structure, not just the number.
