ITR for freelancers & consultants

Self-employed? Multi-client? Overseas income? Here's how to file your ITR cleanly and use the freelancer-friendly presumptive scheme.

🪙 In 60 seconds
  • Most freelancers file ITR-4 (Sugam) using presumptive scheme under Section 44ADA — declare 50% of gross receipts as income.
  • Eligible: specified professionals (doctor, lawyer, engineer, architect, accountant, IT, consultant) with receipts up to ₹75 lakh.
  • Otherwise file ITR-3 with actual P&L and books.
  • Foreign income reportable — claim DTAA / FTC via Form 67.

Section 44ADA — the freelancer's friend

If you're a "specified professional" with receipts up to ₹50L (₹75L if cash receipts ≤ 5%), you can declare 50% as income without keeping detailed books or getting audited. Tax is computed on this 50%.

Who counts as "specified profession"?

  • Legal (advocates)
  • Medical (doctors, surgeons, nursing home professionals)
  • Engineering / architecture
  • Accountancy
  • Technical consulting (IT, software, design, content, marketing)
  • Interior design
  • Information technology professionals
  • Authorised representative / film artist

Example

Freelance designer earns ₹40L in FY 25-26. Under 44ADA:

  • Declared income = 50% × 40L = ₹20L
  • Tax (new regime) ≈ ₹1.6L
  • Tax (old regime, with 80C ₹1.5L) ≈ ₹3.4L on ₹18.5L taxable

No need to keep receipts of every coffee, software subscription, or laptop — that's the simplification of 44ADA.

When ITR-3 makes more sense

If your actual expenses are higher than 50%, ITR-3 with full P&L is better. Example:

  • Freelance video producer with high gear / equipment costs (~70%) → ITR-3
  • Heavy travel / studio rent → ITR-3
  • Multiple employees / contractors paid → ITR-3

Allowable business expenses (ITR-3)

  • Office rent / co-working membership
  • Internet, phone, electricity
  • Software subscriptions (Adobe, Notion, etc.)
  • Equipment depreciation (laptop, camera, etc.)
  • Travel for work
  • Marketing / advertising
  • Professional development (courses, books)
  • Contractor / sub-contractor payments (with TDS)
  • Business meals (50% in some cases)
  • Insurance, bank charges, professional fees

Foreign clients — special considerations

  • Income is fully taxable in India (you're resident)
  • If foreign country withheld tax: claim Foreign Tax Credit via Form 67 (file before ITR)
  • Use DTAA (Double Taxation Avoidance Agreement) — most countries have one with India
  • Get a Tax Residency Certificate (TRC) from your foreign client's country if needed (Form 10F)
  • Report under "Schedule FSI" (Foreign Source Income) and "Schedule TR" (Tax Relief)

Filing flow for freelancers

  1. Compute gross receipts (annual)

    From bank statements, invoices issued. Convert foreign currency at SBI's TT buying rate on the day of receipt.

  2. Decide ITR-4 (presumptive) or ITR-3 (actuals)

    If actual expenses < 50% of receipts, prefer 44ADA / ITR-4. Else ITR-3.

  3. Claim Form 26AS / AIS TDS

    Clients deduct 10% TDS u/s 194J. Auto-credits in your account.

  4. Pay advance tax

    If tax liability > ₹10K, pay quarterly (15 Jun / Sep / Dec / Mar). Else interest u/s 234B/234C.

  5. File before 31 July

    Salaried & non-audit cases. 31 Oct if tax audit applies (rare for 44ADA filers).

GST + ITR combination for freelancers

  • If GST-registered: report gross receipts as per GSTR-1 (excl GST) in ITR
  • If receipts > ₹20L, GST registration is mandatory (see our GST guide)
  • Keep both records reconciling

Most Indian freelancers should use Section 44ADA. Easier, lower tax in many cases, no audit, no books. Switch to ITR-3 only when actual expenses justify it.
