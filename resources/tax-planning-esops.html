ESOP tax planning — exercise, sell, optimise

Your startup gave you ESOPs. Cool. Tax can take 40% if you handle it wrong, or under 15% if you handle it right. Here's how.

🪙 In 60 seconds
  • Two tax events: Exercise (perquisite — taxed at slab) and Sale (capital gain — STCG 15% or LTCG 10%).
  • Eligible startup ESOPs (DPIIT-recognised) can defer exercise tax for up to 5 years, until sale, or until leaving — whichever earliest.
  • For foreign-parent ESOPs, capital gain is on unlisted foreign share — 20% LTCG with indexation; STCG at slab. No ₹1L exemption.
  • Plan exercise timing to control tax outflow — especially if FMV moves significantly.

Tax event 1 — Exercise (perquisite)

When you exercise: you buy shares at the strike price. The "discount" between FMV and strike is treated as perquisite taxed as salary.

Perquisite = (FMV at exercise) − (exercise price)

Taxed at your marginal slab rate (up to 30% + cess + surcharge). Employer deducts TDS.

Example

Granted 1,000 options at strike ₹100. After 4 years, FMV is ₹500. You exercise.

  • You pay company: 1,000 × ₹100 = ₹1,00,000
  • Perquisite: 1,000 × (500 − 100) = ₹4,00,000
  • Tax at 30% slab: ₹1,20,000 (deducted by employer from your salary)
  • Effective acquisition cost (for capital gains later) = FMV at exercise = ₹500/share

Tax event 2 — Sale (capital gain)

When you sell:

  • Holding > 12 months (listed Indian shares STT paid) → LTCG 10% above ₹1L
  • Holding ≤ 12 months → STCG 15%
  • For unlisted / foreign shares: 24 months holding for LTCG; rates differ

Sale price minus FMV at exercise (your cost basis) = capital gain.

Continuing example

Hold shares 18 months after exercise. Sell at ₹800/share.

  • Sale value: ₹8,00,000
  • Cost basis (FMV at exercise): ₹5,00,000
  • LTCG: ₹3,00,000 — first ₹1L exempt → ₹2L × 10% = ₹20,000 tax

The startup ESOP deferral (Section 192(1C))

From FY 20-21, employees of DPIIT-recognised "eligible startups" can defer perquisite tax on ESOPs until earliest of:

  • 5 years from end of FY of exercise
  • Sale of shares
  • Leaving the employer

This is huge for early-stage startup employees — exercise without immediate cash hit.

Foreign-parent company ESOPs

  • You're a resident Indian working for Indian arm of US/global parent
  • ESOPs are on foreign parent's shares
  • At exercise: still perquisite tax in India based on FMV of foreign shares
  • At sale: unlisted foreign shares → LTCG only after 24 months; 20% with indexation; no ₹1L exemption
  • If foreign tax was also withheld on sale: claim FTC via Form 67 + DTAA
  • Disclose in Schedule FA (foreign assets) — mandatory; non-disclosure can attract Black Money Act penalties
⚠️
Schedule FA reportingIf you hold foreign-parent shares (even ESOPs), Schedule FA in ITR-2/3 is mandatory. Includes value as on 31 Dec of calendar year + during year. Skip this = up to ₹10L penalty per year + prosecution risk. Take it seriously.

Common scenarios to plan

Scenario A — Vested but not exercised; company unlikely to IPO

Defer exercise. Exercising creates illiquid wealth + immediate tax. Wait for liquidity event.

Scenario B — Vested + planning to leave

Exercise before leaving (otherwise options expire). Tax bite is unavoidable — but you keep the shares.

Scenario C — IPO incoming, you have 1000 vested options

Exercise during the IPO window (often a 30-90 day window). Tax at exercise (perquisite). Then 12-month holding starts. Sell after 12 months for LTCG benefit.

Scenario D — Cashless exercise + same-day sale

Both events same day. Perquisite + STCG. STCG = ₹0 if no price move between exercise and sale.

The expensive ESOP mistake: exercising at top of market, then watching the stock drop 50%. You owe perquisite tax on the high FMV. The stock at sale is lower. Net loss after tax. Time exercise carefully.
