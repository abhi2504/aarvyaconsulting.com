GSTR-1, GSTR-3B, GSTR-9 — what they are and when to file

Three letters. Three forms. Three different jobs. Get them right and GST runs like clockwork.

🪙 In 60 seconds
  • GSTR-1 — your sales return. Due by 11th of next month (or 13th for quarterly QRMP).
  • GSTR-3B — your summary return + GST payment. Due by 20th of next month.
  • GSTR-9 — annual return summarising the full year. Due by 31 December of the next FY. Mandatory if turnover > ₹2 cr.
  • Missing any of these = late fees + interest + ITC blocked for your buyers.

How they connect

GSTR-1 — the sales return

This is where you tell the government "here's everything I sold this month" — invoice by invoice for B2B sales, and summary for B2C.

What goes in

  • B2B invoices — full details (invoice number, date, buyer GSTIN, taxable value, tax)
  • B2C large (above ₹2.5L inter-state) — invoice details
  • B2C small — state-wise + rate-wise summary
  • Credit notes / debit notes
  • Exports — with shipping bill details
  • HSN-wise summary of outward supplies

Why it matters beyond you

Your GSTR-1 entries flow into your buyer's GSTR-2B. If you don't file it on time or skip an invoice, your buyer can't claim Input Tax Credit on it. They will chase you. They might switch vendors.

Frequency

  • Monthly — if turnover > ₹5 cr or you opted out of QRMP
  • Quarterly (QRMP) — for turnover up to ₹5 cr. Pay tax monthly via PMT-06, but file GSTR-1 only quarterly. Saves time.

GSTR-3B — the summary return + payment

This is the "settle up" return. You declare total sales, ITC claimed, and pay any net tax. Filed by 20th of next month (24th for some states in QRMP).

Structure of GSTR-3B

TableWhat goes there
3.1Outward supplies (sales) — taxable, zero-rated, nil-rated, exempt
3.1.1Supplies notified u/s 9(5) (e-commerce operators)
3.2Inter-state supplies to unregistered / composition / UIN holders

💡
Pro tipGSTR-3B is now mostly auto-populated from GSTR-1 (your sales) and GSTR-2B (your purchases). Verify, don't blindly accept. The "edit" option is there for a reason.

GSTR-9 — the annual return

One return per year that consolidates everything you filed across 12 GSTR-1s and 12 GSTR-3Bs. Plus reconciliation with your books.

Who must file

  • Mandatory: all regular taxpayers with turnover > ₹2 cr
  • Optional: turnover ≤ ₹2 cr (most small businesses)
  • GSTR-9C (Reconciliation Statement): additionally required if turnover > ₹5 cr — needs self-certification

Due date

31 December of the next financial year. (FY 24-25 → due 31 Dec 2025.)

Late fees and interest

  • GSTR-1 late fee: ₹50/day (₹20/day if nil), capped at ₹5,000
  • GSTR-3B late fee: ₹50/day (₹20/day if nil), capped at ₹5,000–₹10,000 per Act (CGST + SGST stack)
  • Interest on late tax payment: 18% p.a. on net cash liability
  • GSTR-9 late fee: ₹200/day (₹100 CGST + ₹100 SGST), capped at 0.5% of turnover
⚠️
The compounding nightmareMiss GSTR-3B → can't file next month's GSTR-3B without filing this one. Returns stack up. Late fees compound. After 6 months of non-filing, GSTIN gets cancelled. Then your input credit history breaks. Don't go there.

Filing checklist (do this every month)

  • By 5th: All previous month's sales invoices entered in books
  • By 8th: All purchase bills entered; cross-check against GSTR-2B on portal
  • By 10th: Reconcile any GSTR-2B mismatches with vendors
  • By 11th: File GSTR-1 (or IFF if QRMP)
  • By 18th: Verify auto-populated GSTR-3B; compute net tax
  • By 19th: Pay GST via PMT-06 challan
  • By 20th: File GSTR-3B and offset payment

GSTR-3B closes the month. GSTR-9 closes the year. Get the monthly habit right and the annual return is just summary work.
