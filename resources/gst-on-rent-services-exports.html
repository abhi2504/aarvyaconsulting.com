GST on rent, services & exports — the special cases

Three common scenarios that confuse most people. Cleared up.

🪙 In 60 seconds
  • Commercial rent — 18% GST. If landlord unregistered, tenant pays RCM.
  • Residential rent — exempt for personal use. But if rented to a GST-registered business, 18% RCM applies on tenant from 18 Jul 2022.
  • Export of services — zero-rated. Either pay IGST & refund, or use LUT.
  • Place-of-supply rules decide IGST vs CGST+SGST split.

GST on commercial rent

If you rent a shop, office, godown or commercial space — GST is 18%, paid by the tenant.

  • Landlord registered → charges 18% on rent, files in GSTR-1, tenant claims ITC
  • Landlord unregistered (or below threshold) → tenant pays GST under reverse charge (RCM) if tenant is registered
  • SAC code: 997212 (renting of commercial space)

Place of supply = location of immovable property. So intra-state rent → CGST+SGST. Tenant's GSTIN must match property state for ITC.

GST on residential rent

This is where it gets interesting.

Old rule (pre-July 2022)

Residential rent for personal dwelling = fully exempt. Anyone, any tenant.

New rule (from 18 July 2022)

  • Residential rent to unregistered tenants for personal use → still exempt
  • Residential rent to GST-registered persons → 18% GST under RCM, payable by tenant (even if for residential use)

Why? To plug a tax-avoidance loophole where companies were renting "residential" units for staff/offices and dodging tax.

⚠️
RCM on residential rent — common misunderstandingThe 18% RCM applies when the tenant is GST-registered. The personal vs business intent of the dwelling doesn't matter. Many small companies that rent flats for staff suddenly have RCM liability.

Exceptions clarified (since Aug 2022)

  • If the registered tenant rents in personal capacity (as proprietor for own dwelling), and is registered as proprietor only — exempt
  • If property is used as residence by employees, RCM still applies

GST on export of services

Recap the "export of service" qualifying conditions (covered fully in LUT guide):

  1. Supplier in India
  2. Recipient outside India
  3. Place of supply outside India
  4. Payment in convertible foreign exchange
  5. Supplier and recipient not the same person

If all 5 ticked → zero-rated supply under Section 16 of IGST Act. Two routes:

Route A — Pay IGST, claim refund

Pay 18% IGST on invoice. File RFD-01 to get refund. Cash blocked for 30–60 days.

Route B — Export under LUT

File annual LUT. Invoice with 0% IGST. Claim refund of input GST on inputs.

Most exporters pick Route B for cash flow.

Place of supply — the cheat sheet

Service typePlace of supply
Immovable property (rent, hotel, accommodation)Location of property
Restaurant, catering, beauty, fitness, trainingLocation of performance
Transportation of goodsLocation of registered recipient (or destination)
Telecommunication, broadcastingLocation of recipient
Banking, insurance, stock-brokingLocation of recipient
OIDAR (online services)Location of recipient
Default rule (B2B)Location of recipient (registered)
Default rule (B2C)Location of supplier

Common combinations & their GST treatment

Indian SaaS company billing US client

Export of service. Zero-rated. Use LUT route. Place of supply outside India.

Indian SaaS company billing Indian B2B client

Place of supply = location of recipient. If same state → CGST+SGST. Different state → IGST.

Pvt Ltd renting flat for CEO's residence

RCM 18% applies on Pvt Ltd. ITC available (since used for employee accommodation in furtherance of business).

Pvt Ltd renting commercial office

Landlord registered → 18% GST on rent → tenant claims ITC. Landlord unregistered → tenant pays under RCM, claims ITC.

Foreign client buys Indian software via subscription

Export of service if all 5 conditions met. Place of supply = client's location.

Special cases in GST aren't loopholes — they're rules with sharp edges. Get them wrong and notices follow. Get them right and they're not actually that complex.

FAQs

Tricky. If the registration uses the residential address and you're renting as a business activity → RCM 18%. If purely residential use as the proprietor with no business use → exempt. Get clarity from a consultant on facts.

Strictly "received in convertible foreign exchange" condition fails. Some advance rulings have allowed it; many haven't. Safer to receive USD directly to bank account (Wise, wire transfer).

Rental of machinery / vehicles (excluding passenger transport) = 18% GST. RCM if landlord unregistered + tenant registered.

Room tariff up to ₹7,500/night → 12%. Above ₹7,500 → 18%. Plus 5% on restaurant component (without ITC for hotel) or 18% (with ITC).

Coworking memberships are services under SAC 997331 → 18% GST. Claim ITC if you're registered.
