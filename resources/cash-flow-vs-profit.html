Cash flow vs profit — the founder gap

"We're profitable but we can't pay salaries this month." If you've said this — read on. You're not alone, and there's a fix.

🪙 In 60 seconds
  • Profit = revenue minus expenses (on paper, when booked).
  • Cash flow = actual money entering / leaving your bank account.
  • A profitable business runs out of cash when: customers pay late, inventory ties up money, GST/TDS payouts hit before customer receipts, or growth eats working capital.
  • The fix: track cash conversion cycle, shorten receivables, lengthen payables (carefully), keep 3 months of runway.

The cookbook example

Imagine you run a sweet shop. In January:

  • You buy ingredients worth ₹1,00,000 (paid cash)
  • You make sweets, sell them to a corporate gifting client for ₹1,50,000 — but they'll pay you in 60 days
  • You spent ₹20,000 on rent, salaries, electricity

Profit for January = ₹1,50,000 − ₹1,00,000 − ₹20,000 = ₹30,000 profit. Your CA's books say you made money.

Cash position = − ₹1,20,000 (₹1L ingredients + ₹20K opex out, nothing came in). Your bank is empty.

You're "profitable" but you can't pay February salaries. This is the gap.

The 4 places cash hides

  1. Accounts receivable — money customers owe you but haven't paid yet
  2. Inventory — raw material or finished goods sitting in the warehouse
  3. Work-in-progress — services you've delivered but haven't invoiced
  4. Advances paid — money you paid suppliers for goods/services not yet received

The cash conversion cycle (CCC)

The metric every founder should know:

CCC = Days you hold inventory + Days customers take to pay − Days you take to pay suppliers

Example: hold inventory 30 days + customers pay 60 days − you pay suppliers in 45 days = 45-day cycle. That means you need to fund 45 days of operations before any sale's money returns to you.

IndustryTypical CCC
SaaS / digital services (annual prepay)− 30 to 0 days (negative = customers pay before you spend)
Services / consulting30–60 days
E-commerce15–45 days
Retail / kirana30–60 days
Manufacturing60–120 days
Construction / infra120–365 days

How to shrink the gap

1. Shorten receivables

  • Invoice the day work is delivered, not the end of month
  • Offer 1–2% discount for payment within 7 days
  • Charge interest on payments beyond 45 days (especially as MSME — protected under MSMED Act)
  • Make it easy to pay — UPI QR on invoice, multiple payment options
  • Send polite payment reminders at day 7, 15, 30 — automate where possible

2. Manage inventory tighter

  • Track inventory turnover monthly
  • Identify slow-moving SKUs — clear them via discounts
  • Use just-in-time ordering for predictable items
  • Set max stock days per SKU and enforce

3. Stretch payables (without burning suppliers)

  • Negotiate longer payment terms — 30 → 45 → 60 days
  • Pay strategically — meet committed dates, no earlier
  • Build credit reputation before you ask for longer terms

4. Always have 3 months of cash runway

Calculate your monthly burn (all fixed + variable opex). Keep at least 3× that in your account or available credit line. Below 1 month = emergency. 1–3 months = caution. 3+ months = comfortable.

🎯
The cash flow forecast templateBuild a 13-week rolling cash flow forecast. Every Friday, update it. Inflows: customer receipts (by likely date), other income. Outflows: salaries, vendor payments, GST, TDS, rent, EMIs. Closing balance must stay positive in every week. If not, act 4 weeks in advance — not 4 days.

Profit is opinion. Cash is fact. Most businesses don't die because they're unprofitable — they die because they ran out of cash while still being "profitable on paper".
