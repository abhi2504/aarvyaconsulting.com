How to handle a GST notice — without panicking

Notices are not allegations — they're requests for clarification. Respond on time, with facts, and most close without escalation.

🪙 In 60 seconds
  • Don't ignore. Every notice has a response deadline (usually 7–30 days). Missing it leads to ex-parte adverse orders.
  • Read the form code (ASMT-10, DRC-01, REG-17, etc.) — it tells you what's being asked.
  • Respond on the GST portal — never on email/letter for formal notices.
  • For most ITC mismatch notices: clean reconciliation file + reply within 30 days closes the case.

Step-by-step response flow

  1. Find the notice on the portal

    Login to gst.gov.in → Services → User Services → View Notices and Orders. Download the notice PDF.

  2. Read it carefully — twice

    Identify: form code, period covered, specific allegation, response deadline, response form (DRC-06 / REG-18 / etc.), officer details.

  3. Calendar the deadline

    Set a reminder for 5 days before deadline. Missing GST notice deadlines = ex-parte order = costly to undo via appeal.

  4. Gather facts & reconciliation

    Most notices want reconciliation. Build:

    • Books vs GSTR-1 vs GSTR-3B vs GSTR-2B for the period
    • Invoice-level support for any disputed entry
    • Vendor confirmations if ITC is questioned

  5. Draft the reply

    Structure: cover letter on letterhead → point-by-point response to each allegation → annexures (reconciliation, invoices, confirmations). Be factual, polite, no emotion.

  6. File the response on portal

    Use the correct response form: DRC-06 (for DRC-01), REG-18 (for REG-17), etc. Attach all supporting docs (PDFs ≤ 5 MB each).

  7. Track and follow up

    The portal shows status. If officer wants personal hearing, attend (or your authorised representative).

  8. Outcome

    Either: notice dropped (good), demand confirmed (you pay or appeal), or further clarification sought (respond again).

Most-asked clarifications in notices

"GSTR-1 vs GSTR-3B mismatch"

Reason: outward supplies declared in GSTR-1 don't match the summary in GSTR-3B.

Fix: prepare reconciliation showing each invoice in both. Common reasons: late uploads, amendments, credit notes timing.

"GSTR-2B vs GSTR-3B ITC mismatch"

Reason: ITC claimed in 3B is higher than what your vendors filed in their GSTR-1 (reflected in your 2B).

Fix: identify which invoices are missing in 2B. Get vendor to file amendment. Or reverse the excess ITC with interest.

"Non-filing of returns"

File the pending returns immediately with late fees. Then respond with proof.

⚠️
Don't argue, reconcileGST officers process hundreds of notices. They like clear reconciliations and quick responses. Long argumentative replies without numbers escalate cases. Lead with the spreadsheet.

A notice is the start of a conversation, not the end of the world. Respond fast, respond with facts, and most close cleanly.

Escalation path

  1. Order under Section 73/74 — adverse demand confirmed
  2. Appeal to Appellate Authority (Form APL-01) — within 3 months, with 10% pre-deposit of disputed tax
  3. Appellate Tribunal (when constituted) — next level
  4. High Court — on questions of law
  5. Supreme Court
