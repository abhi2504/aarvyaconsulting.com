Input Tax Credit (ITC) — claim it right

ITC is the single biggest lever in GST. Get it wrong and you pay tax twice. Get it right and your effective tax drops dramatically.

🪙 In 60 seconds
  • ITC = the GST you paid on purchases, which you can offset against the GST you collected on sales.
  • Net GST you pay = output GST (on sales) − ITC (on purchases).
  • 4 conditions: valid invoice, goods/services received, tax actually paid by vendor, your GSTR-3B filed.
  • What's blocked: motor vehicles (mostly), food/beverages, club memberships, personal-use goods.
  • Strict rule: must match with GSTR-2B. No match = no credit.

The cookbook example

You're a bakery. In a month:

  • You bought flour, oil, packaging worth ₹1,00,000 + ₹18,000 GST = ₹1,18,000
  • You sold cakes worth ₹2,00,000 + ₹36,000 GST = ₹2,36,000

Without ITC: you pay ₹36,000 to govt. Your supplier paid ₹18,000 already. Govt got ₹54,000 on the same value-add chain — double tax. Bad.

With ITC: you offset the ₹18,000 you already paid. Net cash to govt = ₹36,000 − ₹18,000 = ₹18,000. Fair.

The 4 conditions for claiming ITC

  1. Valid tax invoice

    Vendor's invoice must show their GSTIN, your GSTIN, taxable value, tax rate, tax amount, HSN. No proper invoice = no credit.

  2. Goods or services actually received

    Paper invoices without delivery don't qualify. For services delivered over time, partial ITC as it accrues.

  3. Tax paid by vendor to government

    You only get ITC when your vendor has filed their GSTR-1 and the invoice reflects in your GSTR-2B. This is the famous "vendor compliance" problem.

  4. Your GSTR-3B is filed

    ITC is claimed via Table 4 of GSTR-3B. No filing = no claim. Must be claimed within the same FY or by GSTR-3B of November of next FY, whichever is earlier.

What CAN'T you claim ITC on (blocked credits under Sec 17(5))

  • Motor vehicles — except if you're in vehicle trading, transport business, or driving school
  • Food & beverages, outdoor catering, beauty treatment, health services — unless used to make same-category outward supplies
  • Membership of clubs, gyms, health centres
  • Rent-a-cab, life insurance, health insurance — unless mandated by law or for same-category supplies
  • Goods/services for personal consumption
  • Goods lost, stolen, written off, given as gifts/samples
  • Construction of immovable property (other than plant & machinery) for own use

Rule 36(4) — the 100% match rule

From January 2022, you can only claim ITC that appears in your GSTR-2B. Earlier you could claim up to 105% of GSTR-2B (10% provisional). Now zero provisional. If vendor hasn't uploaded, you can't claim.

Practically: chase vendors who file late. Hold payments till you see their invoices in your 2B. Some companies have an internal policy of "10% of bill amount held until 2B match".

⚠️
The vendor riskIf your vendor doesn't pay GST to govt, you lose ITC. Even if you have a perfect invoice and paid them. Welcome to GST's vendor-chasing era — pick reliable vendors with GST track records.

Reverse charge mechanism (RCM)

In some cases the buyer pays GST instead of the seller. Common cases:

  • Import of services
  • Goods Transport Agency (GTA) services
  • Legal services from advocates / firms
  • Director's sitting fees
  • Insurance / banking / business correspondents (specific cases)

You self-invoice, pay GST in cash, and claim it back as ITC (if eligible). RCM tax must be paid in cash — can't offset with existing ITC.

Order of ITC utilisation

From 1 Feb 2019, you must utilise ITC in this order:

  1. IGST credit first — against IGST, then CGST, then SGST liability
  2. CGST credit — only against CGST and IGST liability
  3. SGST credit — only against SGST and IGST liability

Translation: don't let IGST credit pile up. Use it.

GST is not "18% on sales". It's "18% on value addition". ITC is the mechanism that makes it so. Master ITC and you've mastered GST.
