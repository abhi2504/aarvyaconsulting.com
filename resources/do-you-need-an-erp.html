Do you need an ERP? A founder's reality check

If you're juggling Tally, 4 spreadsheets, WhatsApp groups and an "inventory sheet" maintained by Rakesh — yes, you probably do. But not always.

🪙 In 60 seconds
  • ERP = one connected system that runs finance, inventory, sales, purchases, payroll and reporting together.
  • You probably need an ERP if you cross ~₹10 crore turnover, multiple locations, manufacturing or 30+ employees.
  • Sub-₹10 cr service businesses are usually fine with Zoho Books / Tally + add-ons.
  • Realistic ERP rollout cost in India: ₹6L–₹50L over 4–9 months. Annual licence ₹50K–₹15L.

What is an ERP, really?

Think of your business as a body. Right now, you might have:

  • Tally for accounting (the heart)
  • An Excel inventory sheet (the stomach)
  • Your sales team's CRM (the eyes)
  • WhatsApp groups for purchases (the ears)
  • A separate payroll tool (the legs)

Each one is a healthy organ. But they don't talk to each other. Your sales team doesn't know what's in stock. Your accounting team doesn't know what's been ordered. You can't see real-time profitability.

An ERP (Enterprise Resource Planning) is the nervous system that connects all these organs. One database. One source of truth. Every team sees the same numbers.

The 5 signs you've outgrown your current setup

Sign 1 — You can't answer "how much did we make last month" in 5 minutes

If your monthly P&L involves consolidating files from 3 people and waiting 10 days, that's an ERP problem. ERPs give real-time P&L the moment month-end closes.

Sign 2 — Inventory doesn't match books

Your stock register says 1,200 units. The warehouse counts 1,047. The books have 1,189. Nobody knows which is right. Multiple sources of truth = no source of truth.

Sign 3 — You can't compute true product/customer profitability

Which SKU is your most profitable? Which customer eats your margins in service costs? In Tally-only setups, you can't tell. ERPs allocate costs accurately by dimension.

Sign 4 — Approvals are stuck in WhatsApp / email

Purchase orders waiting on the founder. Invoices held up for verification. Vendors angry. ERPs have workflow engines — approvals happen on a phone, logged, time-stamped, auditable.

Sign 5 — Audits and bank loans become forensic exercises

When the audit team or your bank asks "show me trial-to-stock-to-invoice traceability", a Tally + Excel setup means weeks of manual reconciliation. ERPs export this in 1 click.

ℹ️
Reality checkYou don't need an ERP if you're under ₹5 crore turnover, single location, services-only, no inventory. Stay on Zoho Books / Tally + a clean process. ERP at this stage is over-engineering.

ERP doesn't fix bad processes. It calcifies them. Get your operations clean first, then digitise them — never the other way around.

Common pitfalls

⚠️
Pitfall 1 — implementing too earlyDoing ERP at ₹3 cr turnover with 2 founders touching everything = ₹15L sunk cost, no daily benefit, and the system goes unused. Wait until the pain is real.
⚠️
Pitfall 2 — picking software before fixing processBad workflow + expensive ERP = worse outcome than bad workflow + Excel. Map your processes first. We do this in our SOP & process consulting.
⚠️
Pitfall 3 — choosing vendor before knowing your needs"D365 because Microsoft" or "SAP because everyone does" are bad reasons. Different ERPs fit different sizes / sectors. We help with the diagnosis stage before committing.
