Filing your first ITR — without the panic

It's free, it's online, it takes 30 minutes. Here's exactly what to do.

🪙 In 60 seconds
  • Most salaried people file ITR-1 (income from salary, one house property, other sources like FD interest).
  • Deadline: 31 July (for non-audit cases). Late filing fee: ₹1,000–₹5,000 under Section 234F.
  • What you need: Form 16, Form 26AS, AIS, bank statements, investment proofs.
  • Where: incometax.gov.in — free, no charges.

Do you actually need to file?

You must file ITR if any of these is true:

  • Your gross income (before deductions) exceeds ₹2.5 lakh (₹3 lakh for senior citizens, ₹5 lakh for super seniors) — old regime basic exemption
  • You spent more than ₹2 lakh on foreign travel
  • You deposited more than ₹1 crore in current accounts or ₹50 lakh in savings accounts during the year
  • Your electricity bill in a year exceeded ₹1 lakh
  • You're claiming a TDS refund (tax was cut but you owe less)
  • You have foreign assets / income
  • You earned even ₹1 from capital gains (shares, MF, crypto)
💡
File even if you don't have toFiling creates a record of your income — useful for loans, visas, credit cards. And if TDS was deducted, you might be owed a refund.

FormUse it if you have…
ITR-1Salary + 1 house property + other sources (FD, savings interest). Income up to ₹50 lakh. Most salaried first-timers.
ITR-2Above + capital gains (shares/MF/crypto/property), multiple house properties, foreign assets, agricultural income > ₹5,000. No business income.
ITR-3Business or professional income (real books of accounts) — partners in firms, full-time freelancers above presumptive limit.
ITR-4Presumptive scheme — small businesses (44AD), professionals (44ADA), freight (44AE). Income up to ₹50 lakh.

Documents you'll need

  • PAN and Aadhaar (linked)
  • Form 16 from each employer
  • Form 26AS — download from TRACES (see our guide)
  • Annual Information Statement (AIS) — from incometax.gov.in
  • Bank statements (all savings + current accounts)
  • Interest certificates from FDs / recurring deposits
  • Investment proofs: PPF, ELSS, LIC premium, health insurance, home-loan interest certificate, donation receipts
  • Rent receipts (for HRA — old regime)
  • Capital gains statements from your broker / mutual fund

Step-by-step — filing ITR-1

  1. Login to the IT portal

    Visit incometax.gov.in. Click Login. Use PAN as user ID. First time? Click Register first.

  2. Pre-fill from AIS / 26AS

    Go to e-File → Income Tax Returns → File Income Tax Return. Pick AY (Assessment Year — current year is FY-1). Pick mode Online.

    Most fields auto-fill from AIS — salary, TDS, interest, dividends. Verify each line against your Form 16 and bank statements.

  3. Pick regime: Old or New

    This is the most important decision. New regime has lower slabs but barely any deductions. Old regime has higher slabs but lets you claim 80C, HRA, home-loan interest etc. See our old vs new regime guide.

  4. Add deductions (old regime only)

    80C investments (PPF, ELSS, LIC, principal of home loan, EPF, tuition fees — max ₹1.5L)

    80D (health insurance — ₹25K self, ₹50K parents senior)

    80G (donations), 80E (education loan interest), 80EEA (additional home-loan interest)

    HRA exemption, standard deduction (₹50K — auto)

  5. Verify tax computation

    The portal computes your final tax. Cross-check: total income, total deductions, taxable income, tax payable, TDS already paid, refund / balance to pay.

    If refund — you'll get it in 1–4 weeks. If balance — pay it via the same portal before submitting.

  6. Submit and e-verify

    Click Submit. Then e-verify within 30 days using Aadhaar OTP / net banking / bank account EVC. Without verification, the return is invalid.

⚠️
The 30-day verification trapPeople submit ITR and forget to verify. The return remains "filed but not verified" — legally invalid. Verify within 30 days of submission or your filing is dead.

Belated return (with late fee)31 December of AY
Updated return (ITR-U)Up to 24 months after end of AY

Late filing fees (Section 234F)

  • If total income > ₹5 lakh: ₹5,000 late fee
  • If total income ≤ ₹5 lakh: ₹1,000 late fee
  • If income below taxable limit: no fee, but lose right to carry forward losses

Filing on time isn't about avoiding the late fee. It's about keeping the option to claim refunds, carry forward losses, and prove income for the next 8 years.
