Do you need a GST number? A simple 3-question test

Forget the legal language. Answer three yes/no questions and you'll know.

🪙 In 60 seconds
  • You DO need GST if turnover > ₹40L (goods) or ₹20L (services), OR you sell across states, OR you sell on Amazon/Flipkart/Meesho, OR you supply to GST-registered buyers.
  • You DON'T need GST if you're a small local services business / freelancer earning under ₹20L who deals only with end customers.
  • Voluntary GST is often smart for B2B — your buyers prefer it and you can claim input credit.

The simple version

Think of GST like a toll booth on a highway. Every business that adds value pays a little, and the government refunds the part you've already paid (input credit). Whether you need to be in this system depends on three things: how much you sell, who you sell to, and where you sell.

When GST is mandatory regardless of turnover (Section 24)

Even if you're at ₹0 turnover, you must register if you fall in any of these categories:

  • You make inter-state supplies of taxable goods (selling outside your own state — services have a separate ₹20L threshold first)
  • You sell on e-commerce platforms — Amazon, Flipkart, Meesho, Myntra, Zomato, etc.
  • You're an e-commerce operator yourself
  • You're a casual taxable person (occasional supplier in another state — exhibitions, fairs)
  • You're an NRI taxable person
  • You're required to pay tax under reverse charge
  • You're a non-resident OIDAR service provider
  • You're an Input Service Distributor (ISD) or agent of a supplier
⚠️
Watch out — e-commerce sellersSelling on Amazon or Flipkart? GST is mandatory from your very first sale. Doesn't matter if you earn ₹500 or ₹5 lakh. The platforms won't even let you list without a GSTIN.

Should you take voluntary GST?

Even if you're below the threshold, voluntary registration can make sense if:

  • You sell to other businesses (B2B): Your GST-registered buyers prefer suppliers with GSTINs so they can claim input credit. Without one, you'll lose deals to competitors who have GST.
  • You buy a lot of inputs with GST on them: Office rent (commercial), software subscriptions, equipment, raw materials — you can claim back the GST you've paid as input credit.
  • You export goods or services: GST registration unlocks zero-rated supply and refunds on input credit. Major cash benefit for exporters.
  • You're building credibility: Some larger clients won't even consider a vendor without a GSTIN.
💡
When voluntary GST hurtsIf you sell only to end consumers (B2C), adding 18% GST on your bills can make you more expensive than unregistered competitors. Think twice before going voluntary in a price-sensitive B2C niche.

What happens if you should have registered but didn't?

  • Penalty: ₹10,000 or 10% of tax due (whichever is higher), in addition to the tax you should have paid
  • No input credit: You can't claim back the GST you've already paid on purchases during the un-registered period
  • Interest: 18% per annum on the unpaid GST
  • Loss of customers: Once buyers find out you're not GST-compliant, you become unreliable for B2B deals

The ₹50,000 you "saved" by not registering for GST often costs ₹2 lakh when the department comes calling. Register early, sleep well.

Real-world examples

Example 1 — Freelance designer earning ₹15L

Works only with Indian clients (mix of B2B and B2C). Below ₹20L services threshold. GST not mandatory. But if most clients are B2B agencies, voluntary GST is smart — they'll prefer her over un-registered competitors.

Example 2 — Kirana store in Mumbai with ₹30L turnover

Local store, only walk-in customers, sells goods only. Below ₹40L threshold for Maharashtra. GST not mandatory. Voluntary registration probably not worth it — B2C customers don't care about ITC.

Example 3 — Apparel seller on Amazon, ₹8L turnover

Selling on e-commerce platform. Section 24 kicks in. GST mandatory from sale #1.

Example 4 — SaaS startup serving US clients only

Export of services, zero Indian revenue. Below ₹20L Indian turnover. GST not mandatory — but voluntary is highly recommended to claim refund on input GST (servers, software, salaries support).
