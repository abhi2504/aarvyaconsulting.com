Old vs New tax regime — which one wins for you?

The honest answer depends on one number: how much you deduct. Here's how to find your break-even.

🪙 In 60 seconds
  • New regime — lower slabs, but you give up almost all deductions (no 80C, no HRA, no home-loan interest on self-occupied).
  • Old regime — higher slabs, but lets you claim 80C (₹1.5L), HRA, home-loan interest, 80D, etc.
  • Rule of thumb: if your total deductions exceed ~₹3.75 lakh, old regime wins. Below that, new regime usually wins.
  • New regime is the default from FY 23-24. You must actively opt for old if you want it.

What new regime gives up

The new regime drops 70+ exemptions and deductions. The big ones you can't claim:

  • Section 80C (₹1.5L) — PPF, ELSS, EPF, LIC, principal of home loan, tuition fees
  • Section 80D — health insurance
  • HRA exemption
  • LTA (Leave Travel Allowance)
  • Home loan interest on self-occupied property (let-out is OK)
  • Section 80E (education loan interest), 80G (donations)
  • Standard deduction under new regime is ₹75K (up from ₹50K under old)
  • NPS employer contribution under 80CCD(2) is still allowed in both

Worked example: ₹10 lakh salary

Case A: Low deductions (only ₹50K 80C + ₹25K 80D)

The math doesn't decide for you — your lifestyle does. Big home loan + rent + investments? Old wins. Simple salary, no big deductions? New wins.

The break-even rule

For a salaried person at ₹10–15L gross, the rough break-even on total deductions (incl. 80C, 80D, HRA, home-loan interest):

  • Total deductions under ₹3.75L → new regime usually wins
  • Total deductions above ₹3.75L → old regime usually wins
  • Total deductions around ₹3.75L → run both calculations; difference will be small
🎯
Pro tipIf you're under ₹7L total income, the new regime gives a Section 87A rebate that makes your tax practically zero. New is the obvious pick.

FAQs
