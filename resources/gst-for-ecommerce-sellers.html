GST for e-commerce sellers — Amazon, Flipkart, Meesho

Selling online? GST is mandatory from your first sale. Threshold doesn't apply. Here's the full picture.

🪙 In 60 seconds
  • GST is mandatory from first sale for e-commerce sellers — no ₹20L / ₹40L threshold.
  • Each state where you store inventory (FBA) needs its own GSTIN.
  • Marketplace collects 1% TCS — you claim it back via GSTR-2X.
  • Returns flow: GSTR-1 (your sales) ↔ marketplace MIS reconciliation ↔ GSTR-3B.

What's special about e-commerce GST?

Selling through a marketplace (Amazon, Flipkart, Meesho, Myntra, Nykaa, Ajio) brings unique GST obligations:

  1. Mandatory registration from sale #1 (Section 24)
  2. TCS — marketplaces collect 1% tax (0.5% CGST + 0.5% SGST or 1% IGST) on net sales and deposit it as GST in your name
  3. Multi-state GSTIN if you store inventory in marketplace fulfilment centres (Amazon FBA, Flipkart Smart Fulfilment) across states
  4. Daily reconciliation between marketplace MIS, your invoicing, GST returns

Amazon's Fulfilment by Amazon (FBA) and similar programs store your goods in warehouses across India. Each storage state = one GSTIN.

TCS — how it works

From Oct 2018, marketplaces deduct 1% TCS on net taxable supplies (gross sales minus returns) per month, and deposit it as GST in your name.

  1. Marketplace deducts TCS at source

    Visible on your settlement / payout report. Amazon shows it under "TCS GST".

  2. Marketplace files GSTR-8 by 10th of next month

    The TCS gets credited to your Electronic Cash Ledger.

  3. You verify in GSTR-2X

    Marketplace TCS auto-populates in your GSTR-2X — accept / modify.

  4. You claim TCS in GSTR-3B

    Adjusts against your output tax. Treat it like advance tax already paid.

Your monthly e-commerce compliance routine

  • Download settlement reports from each marketplace
  • Reconcile orders → invoices → GSTR-1
  • Check returns / refunds are credit-noted
  • Verify TCS in GSTR-2X
  • Reconcile FBA / inventory movement across states for stock transfers
  • File GSTR-1 by 11th, GSTR-3B by 20th, per state

Common mistakes1. Wrong HSN on listings → incorrect tax → notices. 2. Forgetting to issue credit notes on returns → over-reporting sales. 3. Mismatch between marketplace MIS and your GSTR-1 → notices via DRC-01C. 4. Not registering in FBA-state → invalid invoices, ITC denied for buyers.

E-commerce GST is a data game. The seller with cleanest reconciliation wins — fewer notices, faster refunds, less surprise tax.
