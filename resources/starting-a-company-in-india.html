Starting your own company in India — a beginner's guide

Pvt Ltd, LLP, OPC or Proprietorship? Real costs, real documents, real steps. No jargon, no missing parts.

🪙 In 60 seconds
  • Pick a structure first: Pvt Ltd for fundraising / serious growth, LLP for service firms, OPC for solo founders who still want limited liability, Proprietorship for the simplest possible setup.
  • Realistic cost for Pvt Ltd: ₹7,000–₹15,000 all-in (government fees + a CA's help). Proprietorship: under ₹2,000.
  • Time: 8–12 working days from document collection to Certificate of Incorporation.

Who this guide is for

You're starting something — maybe a freelance design practice, a small trading business, a SaaS startup, a kirana online, a manufacturing unit. You've heard words like "Pvt Ltd", "LLP", "DSC", "ROC" thrown around and it feels overwhelming.

This guide gets you from confused to confident in about 10 minutes of reading.

The simple version

In India, a "company" isn't one thing — it's a category. There are five main flavours, and the right one depends on (a) how serious / large you plan to get, (b) whether you have co-founders or are flying solo, and (c) whether you ever want outside investment.

Think of it like choosing what to ride to work:

  • Bicycle (Sole Proprietorship) — cheap, easy, no paperwork. But if you crash, you crash.
  • Auto-rickshaw (Partnership) — share the ride with someone. Faster but still exposed.
  • Sedan (LLP) — comfortable, protective, fine for most everyday journeys.
  • SUV with airbags (Pvt Ltd) — heaviest, costs more to maintain, but if anything goes wrong you and your passengers are protected. Also the only one investors will get into.
  • Solo SUV (OPC) — Pvt Ltd-grade protection, but built for one driver.

Step-by-step — registering a Pvt Ltd

Most readers want this one, so we'll walk through it. The flow is similar for LLP and OPC with small differences.

  1. Get a Class-3 Digital Signature Certificate (DSC) for every director

    The DSC is your e-signature. The MCA portal won't accept anything without it. Every director (and the subscribers of the MOA) needs one. mca.gov.in lists approved providers.

    Cost: ₹1,200–₹1,800 per DSC. Time: Same day to 2 days.

  2. Reserve a unique company name (SPICe+ Part A)

    Suggest 2 names in order of preference. Names ending in "Private Limited" must be unique and not violate any trademark. Check on the MCA name search before applying.

    Cost: ₹1,000 government fee. Time: 1–3 working days.

  3. Apply for DIN — happens inside SPICe+ Part B

    A Director Identification Number (DIN) is auto-allotted to up to 3 first directors when you file SPICe+ Part B. No separate application needed for them.

    Cost: Included. Time: Same as incorporation.

  4. File SPICe+ Part B with MOA, AOA and supporting forms

    This is the main filing — it incorporates your company, allots PAN and TAN, optionally registers for GST, EPFO and ESIC, and opens a current account placeholder (with select banks).

    You'll attach: identity + address proof of directors, registered-office address proof, NOC from owner if rented, MOA and AOA drafts.

    Cost: ₹0 government fee for authorised capital up to ₹15 lakh; stamp duty varies by state (Delhi ≈ ₹510, Maharashtra ≈ ₹1,000, etc.). Time: 3–5 working days for ROC review.

  5. Receive your Certificate of Incorporation (CoI)

    The Registrar of Companies (ROC) issues the CoI digitally — it has your CIN (Corporate Identity Number), incorporation date, and is the legal birth certificate of your company.

    At this point your company also receives PAN and TAN automatically.

    Time: 1–2 working days after Part B approval.

  6. Open the current account & deposit subscribed capital

    Walk into any bank with your CoI, MOA, AOA, PAN, board resolution and KYC docs. Open a current account in the company's name and deposit the subscribed capital (e.g. ₹1 lakh if that's what you committed).

    Time: 2–7 days depending on the bank.

Documents you'll need

  • PAN of each director / partner
  • Aadhaar of each director / partner
  • Passport-size photo of each director
  • Address proof of each director — utility bill / bank statement (not older than 2 months)
  • Registered office address proof — recent electricity bill or rent agreement
  • NOC from the property owner (if rented or in family member's name)
  • Email ID and mobile number for each director

Common mistakes to avoid

⚠️
Mistake 1 — naming the company wrongPicking a name that sounds great but is too close to an existing brand or trademark. The ROC will reject it and you lose your reservation fee. Always check the trademark database before applying.
⚠️
Mistake 2 — choosing Pvt Ltd "to look professional"If you're a 1-person service business with no fundraising plans, Pvt Ltd's annual compliance (~₹10,000–₹15,000 every year for ROC, audit, ITR) will burn you. LLP or Proprietorship is fine.
⚠️
Mistake 3 — forgetting post-incorporation tasksYou're not done at the CoI. You still need: commencement of business (Form 20A within 180 days), auditor appointment (Form ADT-1 within 30 days), bank account, GST if applicable, MSME / Udyam (free!), and the first ROC filings within the year.

The cheapest mistake is to incorporate too early. The most expensive mistake is to wait too long. Get the structure right at month 3, not year 3.
